# South Florida Market Intelligence

**Week of July 20–25, 2026** | Published July 26, 2026

## Headline Trend — The Property File Is Now Part of the Asset

South Florida's property market looked more functional last week, but not more forgiving.

Sales accelerated, Citizens' lower average rates continued to reach new policies and renewals, and there was no South Florida tropical activation. Yet the week's most consequential developments were not broad market headlines. They were document tests: the association records behind a condo loan, the written decision behind a permit exemption, the retail receipt behind a tax refund, the exact policy form behind a flood requirement, and the dated record behind a claim-status response.

The shift is practical. A property's file is no longer just administrative support for the asset. It increasingly affects whether that asset can be financed, insured, repaired, funded, sold, and defended after a loss.

### Why it matters

- **Property owners:** A clean file can shorten financing, insurance, grant, refund, repair, and claim timelines.
- **Real estate professionals:** Document readiness is becoming part of deal strategy, especially for condominiums and properties with recent improvements.
- **Service providers:** The closeout packet is part of the work product. Permits, approvals, product information, receipts, photos, readings, and completion notes should travel with the repair.
- **Public-adjuster industry:** The new 14-day response duty for qualifying written status requests makes visible file control and specific written updates an operating requirement.

## Trend 2 — Insurance Is Calmer, but Eligibility Is More Granular

Citizens' 2026 homeowners multiperil rates are down an average of 8.8%, and the insurer says it entered hurricane season with stronger claims-paying capacity. Those are meaningful stabilization signals. Last week's short binding suspension and July 22 restart, however, showed how quickly a remote Gulf event can create a statewide transaction issue even when South Florida is not in the forecast cone.

The longer-term rules are getting more property- and policy-specific. Citizens must establish commercial clearinghouses by January 1, 2027. Its final personal-lines flood phase also arrives in 2027, but the requirement turns on the exact Citizens policy form rather than a blanket rule for every customer.

The insurance story is moving from broad scarcity toward narrower questions of timing, form, eligibility, total cost, and documentation. Lower average rates do not answer those questions.

### Why it matters

- **Property owners:** Compare declarations, deductibles, exclusions, flood coverage, and roof requirements—not just the renewal premium.
- **Real estate professionals:** Verify the actual carrier's binding status and the exact policy form before using an insurance assumption in a closing timeline.
- **Commercial and association clients:** Start insurance diligence early enough to compare total cost, coverage, deductibles, exclusions, and financial strength when the clearinghouse framework begins.
- **Referral partners:** The useful advisory conversation is becoming more precise: which fact in this property's file changes the insurance outcome?

## Trend 3 — Strong Sales Are Hiding a Financing and Property-Type Divide

June South Florida sales rose 18.6% year over year, million-dollar sales increased 39.7%, and active inventory fell 18%. The upper end remained especially liquid: cash represented 61% of year-to-date million-dollar sales, while 88% of transactions at $10 million or more were all cash.

That momentum does not describe every buyer or building. Freddie Mac's national 30-year mortgage average rose to 6.58% on July 23. Median condo and townhouse prices increased only in Palm Beach County and declined in Miami-Dade, Broward, Martin, and St. Lucie. Beginning August 3, Fannie Mae Limited Review and Freddie Mac Streamlined Review retire for applicable condo loan applications, increasing the importance of project-level records.

Miami-Dade's preliminary taxable value also rose 5.37%, including new construction. It is not a final tax bill, but it reinforces the same carrying-cost divide: purchase price is only one line in the ownership equation.

### Why it matters

- **Buyers and sellers:** A strong regional sales number does not remove mortgage, insurance, flood, association, assessment, or project-review friction.
- **Condo professionals:** Identify the lender's project-review path before financing and inspection deadlines compress. Budget, reserve study, master insurance, repair status, and assessment records may decide whether the loan stays on schedule.
- **Agents and referral partners:** Separate single-family, condo, luxury, and financed-buyer conditions. The market is moving, but it is not moving evenly.
- **Property owners:** Keep assessment notices and tax records separate from replacement-cost evidence; assessed value is not the cost to rebuild.

## Trend 4 — Resilience Capital Exists, but Access Is Administrative

Last week surfaced property-capital opportunities at three very different scales.

Florida's home-hardening sales-tax refund can return up to $500 in tax paid on qualifying impact-resistant doors, garage doors, and windows purchased from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2029. The benefit is only available through a refund, and it depends on property eligibility, qualifying products, forms, and retail receipts.

My Safe Florida Home says grant funding remains available and anticipates accepting eligible grant applications through August, but new inspection applications are paused. Miami-Dade's multifamily Surtax, SHIP, and HOME funding round remains open through August 14. At the regional scale, Broward's flood-resiliency study has advanced to the federal approval track, but construction funding and authorization remain future steps.

The common thread is not simply that money exists. It is that access depends on timing, eligibility, and a complete file.

### Why it matters

- **Homeowners:** If an existing My Safe Florida Home inspection supports a grant application, verify the portal status and deadline now. Preserve taxed retail receipts for qualifying hardening products.
- **Service providers:** Do not promise approval or reimbursement. Match invoices, product ratings, permits, and photos to the program or refund requirement before work closes.
- **Housing and development teams:** Work from Miami-Dade's final RFA and reconcile site, financing, insurance, compliance, schedule, and scope before the August 14 deadline.
- **Property professionals:** Treat Broward's study as a long-term resilience milestone, not a promise of near-term flood protection for a specific property.

## Trend 5 — Ordinary Summer Weather Was the Local Claims Environment

Tropical Storm Bertha stayed a northern Gulf Coast event. South Florida had no tropical watch or warning, yet heat-index readings near or above 103 degrees, scattered thunderstorms, lightning, quick heavy rain, and poor-drainage flooding remained the practical property risks.

The week also exposed an important operational split: South Florida can avoid a local tropical activation while still feeling a statewide insurance consequence. Citizens' binding restriction applied statewide during the Gulf event and was lifted at 2 p.m. on July 22. Meteorology and insurance operations do not always move together.

For property owners, the absence of a named local storm does not remove the need to document a leak, drainage backup, electrical event, or heat-related equipment failure. For professionals, precision preserves credibility: describe the actual local hazard and separately verify carrier operations.

### Why it matters

- **Property owners:** Photograph the source and spread of new damage before permanent cleanup where safe, protect the property from further damage, and retain emergency-work records.
- **Real estate professionals:** Separate local weather conditions from carrier binding rules when managing quotes and closing expectations.
- **Service providers:** Use heat and lightning stops, secure open work, and close every water-related job with source notes, readings where relevant, and time-stamped photos.
- **Public-adjuster industry:** Avoid turning a remote storm into a local threat narrative. Accurate geography is part of trustworthy claims communication.

## Dates to Watch

- **August 3:** Fannie Mae Limited Review and Freddie Mac Streamlined Review retire for applicable condo loan applications; enhanced reserve-study rules begin.
- **August 5, 2 p.m. and 3 p.m.:** Florida DFS hearings on proposed residential and commercial residential property-claim mediation changes. These are proposed changes, not final rules.
- **Through August, while funding remains:** My Safe Florida Home anticipates accepting eligible grant applications tied to completed initial inspections.
- **August 14:** Miami-Dade's FY 2026 Surtax, SHIP, and HOME multifamily RFA closes.
- **January 1, 2027:** Citizens' commercial clearinghouses are due, and the final flood-coverage phase begins for remaining subject personal residential policies.
- **January 4, 2027:** Fannie Mae's 15% minimum annual replacement-reserve allocation begins for applicable Full Reviews.

## Robinhood Judgment

South Florida's market is becoming more functional, not more forgiving.

Pricing is improving in places, sales are moving, and resilience programs are open. But the week's evidence says the practical advantage belongs to the owner, association, agent, or service provider who can make a property easy to underwrite, insure, repair, fund, and defend.

The property file is part of the asset now. Treat it that way.
