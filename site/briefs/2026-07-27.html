South Florida Property & Insurance Industry Brief

July 27, 2026 | Palm Beach, Miami-Dade, Broward, Martin, St. Lucie

Today's signal: Florida's property-insurance market is getting healthier faster than South Florida policyholders are getting relief. OIR's July report shows lower litigation, easier reinsurance pricing, and average homeowners premiums declining in 51 counties. But across Palm Beach, Broward, Miami-Dade, Martin, and St. Lucie, the average wind-included premium fell only about 0.8% to 1.6% between OIR's last two reporting snapshots. South Florida's three largest counties also posted a 27.27% litigated-claim rate, more than triple the 8.16% rate in the rest of the state. The Robinhood read: the statewide trend is finally moving the right way, but a healthier insurance market is not the same thing as an inexpensive renewal or an easy claim. Local outcomes still turn on the actual policy form, property condition, mitigation evidence, and the quality of the claim file.

TOP STORIES

1. South Florida's insurance relief is real, but still modest

OIR's latest county table puts the average traditional homeowners premium with wind at $6,323 in Palm Beach, $6,136 in Broward, $5,975 in Miami-Dade, $5,899 in Martin, and $3,491 in St. Lucie. All five were lower than the September 30, 2025 snapshot used in OIR's January report, but the reductions ranged from just $31 to $94. That is roughly 0.8% to 1.6%, based on Robinhood's calculation from the two OIR tables.

The broader market data is stronger. Personal-residential lawsuit filings were down 25% in the first five months of 2026 compared with the same period in 2025, and preliminary reinsurance data shows nearly half of participating insurers receiving risk-adjusted price reductions of 15% to 25%. Yet claims closed in Palm Beach, Broward, and Miami-Dade had a 27.27% litigation rate, compared with 8.16% in all other counties.

Action: shop the actual renewal with matching limits and deductibles, verify every available mitigation credit, and keep the roof, permit, inspection, maintenance, and improvement records in one property file. After a loss, report promptly and preserve a clean chronology instead of assuming a better statewide market will settle a weak file.

Sources: OIR July 2026 Property Insurance Stability Report, OIR January 2026 Property Insurance Stability Report, Insurance Business

2. Citizens' average rate cut is not a promise for every renewal

Citizens says its July 2026 changes produce an average statewide decrease of 8.8% for homeowners multiperil policies and 5.1% for homeowner wind-only policies. The changes apply to new and renewal policies effective on or after July 1, but the result varies by product, form, and territory.

Common HO-3, HW-2, HO-6, and HW-6 forms have separate caps: primary policies are subject to a maximum 2% decrease and non-primary policies to a 0% cap. Citizens also revised wind-loss mitigation tables for several tenant and condominium-unit forms.

Action: compare the new declarations page against the expiring policy line by line. Confirm the wind-mitigation inspection is current and correctly credited, and do not build a household budget or closing estimate from the statewide average.

Source: Citizens July 2026 rate, rule, and wind-loss mitigation bulletin

3. Miami-Dade's same-day permit program still needs implementation

Miami-Dade announced final approval of a permanent same-day residential permitting program on July 21. The underlying ordinance covers many routine projects, including roofing, windows and doors, pools, fences up to six feet, kitchen and bathroom remodels, and mechanical, electrical, and plumbing repairs.

There are two important brakes. The County's legislative tracker still showed the item as “Before the Board” when checked July 27, and the ordinance says the program must be implemented through a separate order approved by the Board. Projects requiring structural review, properties in historic districts, properties with open code-enforcement cases, and work intended to legalize existing violations are excluded. Once implemented, a department-caused delay can reduce the permit fee by 10% per business day.

Action: treat the program as pending until the County publishes the implementing order and live intake instructions. Do not promise a same-day permit from the press release alone; confirm eligibility and submit a complete electronic package.

Sources: Miami-Dade County announcement, Miami-Dade legislative item 261305

4. Strong cells make Monday a property-protection day

South Florida's Monday setup supports slow-moving thunderstorms with heavy downpours and localized flooding. Local 10 says the strongest cells could become briefly severe, with damaging gusts, frequent lightning, and very heavy rain. CBS Miami expects heat to feel like the upper 90s or 100s before storms arrive and says tropical development is not expected during the next seven days.

Action: secure open roofs, materials, temporary coverings, and loose exterior items before storms build. Stop roof and ladder work when thunder is present. If damage occurs, photograph the source and spread before temporary repairs, keep removed material when safe, save invoices, and report the loss promptly.

Sources: CBS Miami, Local 10

SEGMENT NOTES

  • Homeowners: Use the renewal itself—not a statewide average—to judge insurance relief. Keep mitigation, permit, roof, maintenance, and improvement records together, and document any storm damage before cleanup changes the scene.
  • Service Providers: Do not advertise Miami-Dade's same-day program as live until the implementing order and intake process are published. Build complete permit and closeout files, and secure active work before afternoon storms.
  • Real Estate Professionals: Price insurance from property-specific quotes early in the transaction. Treat the same-day permit program as pending, and verify open permits, code cases, mitigation records, and the exact policy form before relying on a closing estimate.
