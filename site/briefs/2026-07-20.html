South Florida Property & Insurance Industry Brief

July 20, 2026 | Palm Beach, Miami-Dade, Broward, Martin, St. Lucie

Today's signal: Today's signal is a split-screen risk environment: Tropical Depression Two is organizing in the Gulf but is not currently a South Florida landfall story, while Miami-Dade and Broward are under a heat advisory and the local afternoon-storm pattern remains the practical property risk. At the same time, South Florida's housing market is accelerating at the upper end, with June sales up 18.6% year over year and million-dollar sales up 39.7%. The useful business takeaway is not panic; it is readiness: active property values, heavier cooling loads, ordinary storm exposure, and insurance documentation all need to be read together.

TOP STORIES

Tropical Depression Two is a Gulf event, but South Florida still has a live weather-and-claims day

The National Hurricane Center was issuing advisories Monday on Tropical Depression Two in the northeastern Gulf. At the latest NHC update, the depression was drifting north-northwest near 3 mph with 30 mph maximum sustained winds, and the center said the system was expected to strengthen. Florida Disaster reported that the system was not expected to make landfall in Florida, but its broader weather outlook called for scattered showers and storms elsewhere across the Peninsula. The Gulf Coast threat should not be repackaged as a South Florida landfall threat.

Implication: South Florida owners still have a real claims-preparation day, just a different one. The immediate risks are heat-stressed HVAC systems, afternoon lightning and wind, localized heavy rain, roof and opening leaks, drainage backups, and water intrusion that gets misclassified because there is no named storm nearby. The right public message is precise: monitor the Gulf system, follow local alerts, and document ordinary weather damage promptly.

Sources: National Hurricane Center, Florida Disaster weather outlook

Miami-Dade and Broward heat advisory raises the property-maintenance signal

The National Weather Service Miami office issued a heat advisory for Miami-Dade and Broward from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. Monday, with heat-index values expected to reach 104–108 degrees. NWS also expects showers and thunderstorms to develop late this morning and afternoon, favoring inland South Florida, with a strong thunderstorm possible.

Implication: this is an equipment-and-documentation story as much as a health story. Older or heavily loaded HVAC systems, rooftop equipment, pool pumps, electrical components, roof membranes, sealants, and attic spaces are all under stress. Property owners and service providers should record pre-existing conditions, service calls, temperatures or moisture readings where relevant, and any repair scope before the next failure is blamed on a later storm.

Source: National Weather Service Miami

South Florida sales are accelerating, especially where property values and claim complexity are highest

MIAMI REALTORS® and RWorld reported July 17 that June closed sales rose 18.6% year over year for the tenth consecutive month, with year-to-date sales up 7.9%. Million-dollar sales rose 39.7% year over year in June, while year-to-date sales of $10 million or more reached 306 transactions and were 88% cash. Active inventory was down 18% year over year, and Palm Beach, Miami-Dade, Broward, Martin, and St. Lucie all recorded year-to-date single-family sales growth.

Implication: stronger transaction volume creates more property-intelligence and referral opportunities, but it does not make insurance friction disappear. High-value and newly transferred properties need current roof, flood, wind-mitigation, permit, prior-loss, equipment, and water-intrusion documentation. The market is moving faster; the file still needs to be accurate before the next claim or inspection exposes a gap.

Source: MIAMI REALTORS® + RWorld June 2026 market update

Miami office demand is improving, but the improvement is selective rather than a blanket boom

The Real Deal reported July 17 that new-to-market firms signed Miami leases, including Brevan Howard in Coconut Grove, Amcor at Southeast Financial Center, and Quadel in downtown Miami. The report also put Miami-Dade Class A office vacancy at 15.7% in the second quarter, down 10 basis points quarter over quarter and 130 basis points year over year; Class B vacancy was 14%. Average Miami office asking rent was reported at $59.66 per square foot.

Implication: office and mixed-use property activity is improving at the margin, but the underlying asset-management question remains practical: which buildings are retaining tenants, what improvements are being made, and whether mechanical, roof, water, and buildout records are keeping pace with occupancy changes. New leases can create referral opportunities for contractors, property managers, brokers, and insurance professionals who understand both the physical asset and the documentation trail.

Source: The Real Deal - Miami office leases

Citizens has more capacity for a major event, but rate relief is not the same as claim readiness

Citizens reported that it had 278,269 policies in force as of July 10 and secured $2.82 billion in 2026 hurricane-season reinsurance, described as capacity to pay claims after a 1-in-360-year hurricane without levying assessments on Florida insurance consumers. Citizens also reports that its 2026 homeowners multiperil rates are falling an average of 8.8% statewide under rates approved by the Office of Insurance Regulation.

Implication: the market message should stay balanced. Capacity and lower average rates are meaningful developments, but neither one settles a coverage dispute, proves a property's condition, or replaces a complete claim file. Homeowners should retain policy documents, inventories, photos, receipts, mitigation records, and contractor reports before a loss—not after an adjuster asks for them.

Sources: Citizens news and policy count, Citizens 2026 rate announcement

WHAT TO DO TODAY

  • Photograph roof edges, ceilings, windows, doors, exterior drainage, HVAC equipment, and any existing stains before afternoon storms.
  • Save service invoices, moisture readings, repair scopes, and time-stamped photos in one property folder.
  • For a purchase or listing, ask for roof age, permits, wind-mitigation documents, flood information, prior claims, association records, and current insurance terms before relying on the sales price alone.
  • If a loss occurs, document the source and spread of damage before permanent cleanup where reasonably safe, protect the property from further damage, and keep all emergency-work records.
